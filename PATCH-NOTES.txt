MELON LANGUAGE – IOE ARCADE + CHINESE DAILY GOAL VISUAL PATCH

What is included
- Restores the Gemini-style IOE lesson flow: 3 lessons, random session selection of 4 / 6 / 10 mini-games, session timer, score summary, best scores, and lesson unlock progress.
- Keeps the original 10 Melon mini-game modes and adds 29 unique, self-authored IOE-format practice modes (39 selectable activities total).
- Adds Listening and pronunciation formats, odd-one-out, synonyms/antonyms, spelling, grammar, dialogue, reading/cloze, sentence transformation, and the Sailor Rescue task referenced by the original Lesson 3 but missing from the prototype.
- Listening tasks attempt automatic speech playback and retain a replay button as a fallback.
- Adds 10 lightweight local MP4 loop clips (4 seconds, H.264, 640x360) and 10 local WebP illustrations for in-game scenes. The clips are gentle camera-motion loops over illustrations, with additional CSS motion overlays; they are not full character-animation footage. All assets are local and do not rely on a CDN.
- Restores the four quick-play cards to a horizontal layout using isolated CSS classes so IOE art styles cannot override them.
- Adds a small watermelon slice to each Chinese daily-goal card. A goal's fruit brightens and gently bobs when that goal is complete. Existing Chinese tower, daily target, and fruit-stage calculations are otherwise unchanged.

Files are intended to be overlaid onto the root of the existing project. This patch intentionally does not include .git, .env.local, node_modules, .next, or Vercel secrets/configuration. Keep your local .git and .env.local files.

Important content note
The 29 added activities are original practice examples based on common IOE exercise formats. They are not official IOE game titles and do not reproduce official exam questions. A number of new modes currently contain one curated sample prompt each; expanding them into larger randomized question banks is a suitable next iteration.

After applying
Run `npm run build` locally to verify against your installed dependencies. If the project is connected to Vercel through GitHub, pushing the commit may trigger deployment automatically according to that repository's Vercel settings.
